# Mushku theorem-paper design standard

Author: S N Justin Ram  
Affiliation: Mushku Nobleworks  
ORCID: 0009-0008-3593-2816

This standard applies to every theorem paper in the series. Its academic
presentation baseline is the direct technical style exemplified by Vaswani et
al., *Attention Is All You Need*, arXiv:1706.03762.

## Authorship and voice

- The title page, affiliation, ORCID, email, citation metadata, and archive
  record establish authorship.
- The body never announces that the author wrote the paper or theorem.
- Technical prose states the object, construction, law, proof, consequence, or
  boundary directly.
- First person may be used naturally for a methodological statement, but it is
  never inserted merely to establish ownership.
- No assistant, agent, reviewer, approval workflow, "human," or "user" narrates
  the paper.

## Abstract

The abstract states, in order:

1. the technical object or problem;
2. the construction introduced by the paper;
3. the central theorem or exact result;
4. the principal operational or compositional consequence;
5. the precise scope boundary when one is mathematically necessary.

The abstract contains no seal workflow, review history, request for permission,
defense against hypothetical skepticism, implementation diary, or self-
authorship declaration.

## Technical progression

The preferred theorem-paper progression is:

1. problem and exact contribution;
2. construction and typed operators;
3. canonical representation;
4. inverse or closure boundary;
5. calculation or composition interface;
6. theorem statements;
7. proofs at the declared level;
8. exact scope and nonclaims;
9. conclusion and references.

Every named term receives a conventional technical definition at first use.
Tables and diagrams exist only when they materially compress a relationship
that prose would obscure.

Place the principal architecture or lifecycle figure immediately after the
introduction, before detailed mechanics. The figure exposes the forward
dependency of the construction; it does not certify the paper or repeat its
status.

## Theorem fidelity

- The paper states the theorem; it does not debate whether the theorem is
  allowed to exist.
- Every equation, domain, codomain, premise, rejection condition, clamp,
  inverse law, and boundary must match the governing theorem.
- Editorial prose may explain or organize theorem material but may not add,
  weaken, reinterpret, or silently omit theorem content.
- Patent-derived requirements must be traceable without turning the body into
  patent prosecution, legal argument, or governance narration.
- A calculation engine may be named only generically unless a later paper is
  separately authorized for release.
- When its interior is outside scope, use the direct boundary statement:
  "`Calculate_A` is specified by its typed input-output contract and closure
  laws; its internal implementation is not described here."

## Confidence and status language

- Confidence comes from the definitions, theorem, and proof. The paper does
  not certify its own epistemic status.
- Remove statements such as "feasibility is closed," "already proven,"
  "implementation failure can never be theorem failure," or any equivalent
  attempt to rule out contrary evidence through prose.
- Do not add hedging, humility theater, invitations for debate, or requests for
  permission. State the construction and result directly.
- Running headers, title blocks, section names, and related-work prose use
  neutral technical language.
- The words `ratified`, `immutable`, `governing`, `authority`, `seal`,
  `approval`, `normative`, and `authorization` belong only in an exact archival
  artifact or release metadata when they describe an actual lifecycle fact.
  They do not narrate the reader-facing theorem paper.
- Reader-facing prose prefers technical verbs such as `defines`, `determines`,
  `specifies`, and `establishes` over administrative verbs such as `governs`,
  `authorizes`, or `ratifies`.
- `credit`, `receipt-bound`, and `progression` language stays in archival
  material or release metadata unless the theorem itself requires the exact
  term. Proof-only objects are instead described by what they are not:
  execution evidence, admission evidence, or implementation-conformance
  evidence.
- Related work describes mathematical relationship and non-dependence:
  "These constructions are related but are not used in the definition or
  proof." It does not use jurisdictional language.

## Diagram semantics

- Every arrow terminates visibly at its actual codomain.
- A lifecycle diagram depicts the mathematical sequence, not the paper,
  prose, review process, or publication workflow.
- Closed or private components are shown only by their typed boundary.
- Downstream-paper boxes name the downstream theorem application, not
  "academic prose," approval status, or document-production machinery.

## Publication design

- Use a conventional scholarly title page and readable abstract.
- Use restrained section hierarchy, consistent notation, numbered equations
  when referenced, legible tables, embedded fonts, searchable text, and one
  paper size across the series.
- Put hashes, receipts, exact-byte manifests, and archival provenance in a
  compact archival section or supplementary packet when they are required.
- Keep internal agent instructions, red-team workflow, approval dialogue, and
  implementation planning out of the academic narrative.
- When a fixed formal source must be preserved, publish a normal reader-facing
  paper and reproduce the fixed source verbatim in a clearly marked archival
  appendix. Keep the exact source, lifecycle record, and checksums beside the
  PDF in the release packet.

## Verification gate

Each paper must pass all of the following before sealing:

- title-page author identity and citation metadata are correct;
- normal academic prose is used throughout;
- no explicit self-authorship declaration occurs;
- no assistant, agent, reviewer, human/user, or approval-workflow narrator
  occurs outside a clearly marked immutable historical artifact;
- theorem-to-paper equivalence and all paper-specific clamps pass;
- PDF visual, text-layer, font, metadata, page-size, and checksum gates pass;
- the publication packet contains exactly one current PDF and no superseded
  upload authority.

Reference: Ashish Vaswani et al., *Attention Is All You Need*,
arXiv:1706.03762, 2017, https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762.
