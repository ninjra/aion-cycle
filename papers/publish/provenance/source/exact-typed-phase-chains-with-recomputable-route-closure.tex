\begin{abstract}
This paper defines a fixed typed phase chain over the canonical sparse residual
tensor boundary.  The route takes a dense input and a second canonical
operation tensor, crosses an exact opaque transport boundary, performs typed
calculation, crosses back, restores the selected result, and atomically
manifests those exact bytes.  Every transition fixes its epoch, position,
input and output type, operator, evaluator, predecessor, and canonical value
commitments.  Exact continuity is established from canonical typed bytes;
hashes provide separate mutation-detection evidence.  Verification is
streaming and retains no vector of phase values or alternate mapback.  A
bounded active-window construction remains an independent tensor-realization
section rather than an invented bridge into dense restoration.  The result is
one exact route result or typed rejection.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} typed phase chain; route closure; canonical
tensor; exact composition; streaming verification; fail-closed evidence.

\section{Introduction}

An ordered list of stage names is not a typed route.  Exact composition
requires each stage to consume precisely the value produced by its
predecessor, including rejection type and epoch.  It also requires the
canonical calculation operand to remain data rather than becoming an outer
opcode, and it requires present verification instead of a stored success flag.

The construction below fixes one main route and one independent bounded
tensor-realization section.  The main lifecycle is:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{c@{\ }c@{\ }c}
$C\xrightarrow{\Phi_A,\Enc_A}$
&\fbox{data bytes}$\xrightarrow{W^{\rm data}_{\rm in,A}}$
&\fbox{data ingress}\cr
$D_{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\Enc_A}$
&\fbox{operation bytes}$\xrightarrow{W^{\rm op}_{\rm in,A}}$
&\fbox{operation ingress}\cr
\multicolumn{3}{c}{$\downarrow\ \Dec_A\times\Dec_A\quad
\Calc_A(D_{\mathrm{data}},D_{\mathrm{op}})\quad\downarrow$}\cr
\fbox{result bytes}$\xrightarrow{W^{\rm data}_{\rm out,A}}$
&\fbox{result egress}
&\fbox{operation echo}$\xleftarrow{W^{\rm op}_{\rm out,A}}$
  \fbox{operation bytes}\cr
\multicolumn{3}{c}{$\downarrow\ \Dec_A,\Resolve_A,\Manifest_{A,N}$\quad
\fbox{exact published result}}\cr
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Both canonical operands cross complete, separately declared Wire routes.  The
operation route returns an exact unchanged echo; no calculation operand travels
beside or around an opaque crossing.

Origamold supplies a bounded realization of a selected tensor closure.  Its
codomain is a tensor window, not dense restored bytes; it is therefore bound
as an independent section rather than inserted between Resolve and
manifestation.  This distinction is what makes the cross-paper composition
type-correct.

The patent supplies the envelope foundation and fidelity/aperture clamps
\cite{ram2026patent}.  Ionizer supplies the canonical tensor, exact inverse,
selector, and two-operand calculation boundary \cite{ram2026srft}.  Papers
II--IV supply exact transport, bounded tensor realization, and atomic
manifestation respectively \cite{ram2026transport,ram2026window,ram2026manifest}.
The internal method of a calculation engine is outside this paper.

\section{Exact imported signatures}

Fix a valid Ionizer epoch $A$.  Let
\[
\mathcal C_A=U^*,\qquad
\mathcal D_A=\operatorname{CanonicalTensor}_A,
\qquad \mathcal B_A=\operatorname{Image}(\Enc_A).
\]
Set $\Enc_A=\operatorname{CanonicalEncoding}_A$ and
$\Dec_A=\operatorname{CanonicalDecode}_A$.  The imported maps are
\begin{align}
\Phi_A &: \mathcal C_A\to\mathcal D_A,\\
\Enc_A &: \mathcal D_A\to\mathcal B_A,\\
\Dec_A &: \mathbb B^*\to\mathcal D_A\sqcup\{\Reject\},\\
\Calc_A &: \mathcal D_A\times\mathcal D_A
       \to\mathcal D_A\sqcup\{\Reject\},\\
\Resolve_A &: \mathcal D_A\times\operatorname{Selector}_A
       \to\mathcal C_A\sqcup\{\Reject\}.
\end{align}
In particular, Resolve returns bytes.  The history-valued Ionizer operator is
$\mu_A$; this paper does not rename its codomain.

Paper II supplies the exact owner signatures
\begin{align}
 W_{\mathrm{in},A}(b)&=(b_{\mathrm{in}},\rho,d_{\mathrm{in}}),\\
 W_{\mathrm{out},A}(\rho,d_{\mathrm{in}},b_{\mathrm{out}})
  &=(b_{\mathrm{return}},d_{\mathrm{out}}).
\end{align}
The theorem is instantiated twice as two complete routes with data projections
\begin{align}
 W^{\rm data}_{\mathrm{in},A}(b_d)&=W_{\mathrm{in},A}(b_d)
   =(b_d,\rho_d,d_{d,\mathrm{in}}),\\
 W^{\rm data}_{\mathrm{out},A}(\rho_d,d_{d,\mathrm{in}},b_r)
  &=W_{\mathrm{out},A}(\rho_d,d_{d,\mathrm{in}},b_r)
    =(b_r,d_{d,\mathrm{out}}),\\
 W^{\rm op}_{\mathrm{in},A}(b_o)&=W_{\mathrm{in},A}(b_o)
   =(b_o,\rho_o,d_{o,\mathrm{in}}),\\
 W^{\rm op}_{\mathrm{out},A}(\rho_o,d_{o,\mathrm{in}},b_o)
  &=W_{\mathrm{out},A}(\rho_o,d_{o,\mathrm{in}},b_o)
    =(b_{o,\mathrm{echo}},d_{o,\mathrm{out}}),
\end{align}
or typed rejection, with $b_{o,\mathrm{echo}}=b_o$ byte for byte on success.
The data and operation route states are separate surrounding products, not
additional fields in either owner tuple.  The two route/digest states are
distinct and both lifecycles close.  The
Origamold section uses demand $Q=(q,b)$ and profile $R_A$:
\[

\qquad \operatorname{Read}(W_q)=\beta_{A,\Lambda}(D|q),
\]
or typed rejection, where $\operatorname{ConformingProfile}_A(R_A)$ and
$0\le |\beta_{A,\Lambda}(D|q)|\le b\le B_{\rm window}$.  The Energizer boundary is
\[
\Manifest_{A,N}(D,s,t)=
\operatorname{PUBLISHED}(t,|C_s|,H(C_s),B^*,\operatorname{epoch}(D),s)
\]
or rejection, where $C_s=\Resolve_A(D,s)$.  No companion signature uses a
``selected history to window to manifestation'' chain.

\begin{definition}[Route]
The main route is the fixed vector
\[
\begin{aligned}

&\mathsf{ENCODE\_OPERATION},\mathsf{WIRE\_OPERATION\_INGRESS},

\section{Independent bounded tensor section}

For $Q=(q,b)$ and conforming profile $R_A$, the bounded section is
\[
\operatorname{WindowSection}_A(D,Q;R_A)
=\operatorname{Realize}_A(D,Q;R_A)=(W_q,K_q)\quad\text{or }\Reject.
\]
It binds the same epoch and canonical tensor identity as the relevant main
route boundary and requires
$0\le|\beta_{A,\Lambda}(D|q)|\le b\le B_{\rm window}$.  It produces exactly
the window $W_q$ and continuation set $K_q$.  It does not change $D$, does not
return $\Resolve_A(D,s)$, and does not feed $\Manifest_{A,N}$.  Its proof may
be referenced by identity while its values remain outside the main route.

\begin{proposition}[Codomain separation]
Substitution of $W_q$ for $C_s$ is ill typed.  Binding the independent window
section to $D$ preserves both companion contracts without adding a conversion
operator or alternate decoder.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$W_q$ is a layout-bound selected tensor-closure image, whereas $C_s\in U^*$ is
the result of Ionizer selected mapback.  Their defining equations and roles
are distinct.  The separate section uses each at its declared codomain.
\end{proof}

\section{Optional proof section}

A proof section exists only when a proof profile $P$ is fixed in the route
declaration.  Its exact signatures are
\begin{align}
\operatorname{Prove}_{A,P}&:
 (\operatorname{Statement}_{A,P},\operatorname{ProverInput}_{A,P})
 \to\operatorname{ProofBytes}_{A,P}\sqcup\{\Reject\},\\
\operatorname{Verify}_{A,P}&:
 (\operatorname{Statement}_{A,P},\operatorname{ProofBytes}_{A,P})
 \to\operatorname{VerifiedStatement}_{A,P}\sqcup\{\Reject\}.
\end{align}
Without $P$, neither phase is present, neither a profile nor a prover value is
part of $X_0$, and Close consumes $X_{13}$ directly.
With $P$, the exact suffix is
\begin{align*}
X_{14,P}&=\operatorname{ManifestReceipt}_{A,N}\times
 \operatorname{Statement}_{A,P}\times\operatorname{ProofBytes}_{A,P},\\
X_{15,P}&=\operatorname{ManifestReceipt}_{A,N}\times
 \operatorname{VerifiedStatement}_{A,P},\\
X_{16,P}&=\operatorname{RouteResult}_{A,N}.
\end{align*}
Prove derives the canonical statement from the route identity, manifestation
link digest, and fixed claim, then consumes the prover input derived from the
fixed profile; it is not a free caller value.
Verify produces the verified statement, and Close consumes it with the receipt.
The profile, derived prover input, and both proof phases are route-identity
bound.  Any partial, reordered, duplicated, or profile-free proof suffix
rejects.
The route binds the selected proof relation; it does not extend the soundness
claim of the proof system or its setup \cite{groth2016}.

\section{Canonical identities and exact equality}

For each phase type $X$, fix one canonical encoding.  Define
\begin{align}
\operatorname{type\_id}_A(X)
 &=H(\mathtt{AION\mbox{-}TYPE\mbox{-}V2}\|A\|\Canonical(X)),\\
\operatorname{value\_id}_A(X,x)
 &=H(\mathtt{AION\mbox{-}VALUE\mbox{-}V2}\|A\|
   \operatorname{type\_id}_A(X)\|\Canonical_X(x)).
\end{align}
Exact phase and adjacency equality is byte equality of canonical typed values,
or identity of the same immutable typed boundary object.  Digest equality is
not an equality proof.  SHA-256 commitments provide mutation evidence only
under their collision and second-preimage assumptions \cite{fips1804}.

The derived route identifier binds the epoch, entrypoint, route vector, all
phase declarations, the exact canonical operation operand, the declared data
and operation Wire route slots, selector, target namespace, and optional proof
profile.  The concrete route and digest states produced at both crossings are
present in the typed values and therefore in the applicable link commitments.
The caller cannot replace any of those values with a phase label or outer opcode.
Its exact domain prefix is \texttt{AION-ROUTE-V2}; route, type, value, link,
chain-start, and link-digest bodies use only their stated V2 domains.  A V1,
V3, unversioned, or unhashed-type body is distinct and rejects.

\section{Link construction}

For admitted $\operatorname{Eval}_A[i](x_i)=\OK(x_{i+1})$, define the canonical
link body
\[
\begin{split}
L_i=\CanonicalRecord(&\mathtt{AION\mbox{-}TYPED\mbox{-}LINK\mbox{-}V2},A,
e_A,\rho_A,\operatorname{phase}_i,i,n,\\
&\operatorname{type\_id}_A(X_i),\operatorname{type\_id}_A(X_{i+1}),
o_i,\eta_i,h_{i-1},\\
&\operatorname{value\_id}_A(X_i,x_i),
\operatorname{value\_id}_A(X_{i+1},x_{i+1}),\OK),
\end{split}
\]
with
\[
h_{-1}=H(\mathtt{AION\mbox{-}CHAIN\mbox{-}START\mbox{-}V2}\|\rho_A\|A\|e_A),
\qquad
h_i=H(\mathtt{AION\mbox{-}TYPED\mbox{-}LINK\mbox{-}DIGEST\mbox{-}V2}\|L_i).
\]
There is no extension field.  $L_i$ contains declarations and commitments,
not a phase value, source handle, dictionary, pocket, source-record identifier,
or restoration recipe.

\section{Streaming verification}

Link verification receives only the current exact input, current exact output,
one link, and the validated predecessor.  It strictly parses the record,
checks all declarations, compares canonical bytes, freshly evaluates the
declared relation, recomputes the value commitments, and recomputes the link
digest.  The evaluator binding is derived from the actual declared callable
and checked with its fixed epoch, entrypoint, route identity, route input, and
canonical phase position; callable or evaluator-environment substitution under
an unchanged label rejects.  Link and chain verification
return verified evidence or typed rejection rather than Boolean truth.

Timeout, unavailability, callable-identity mismatch, exception, malformed
input, and semantic failure are distinct typed rejection results.  None creates
a successful link or escapes the aggregate result algebra.

Whole-chain verification accepts
$(C,D_{\mathrm{op}},s,t)$, the ordered links, the terminal result, and expected
terminal digest.  It does \emph{not} accept a boundary-value vector.  It keeps
at most $x_i$, $x_{i+1}$, $h_{i-1}$, and explicitly bounded operator state;
after link $i$ verifies, consumed $x_i$ is released.  Thus link evidence
cannot become a retained restoration ledger.

\begin{lemma}[Streaming continuity]
If link $i$ verifies and phase $i+1$ begins, the canonical typed input of phase
$i+1$ equals the exact output of phase $i$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The verifier supplies the freshly evaluated $x_{i+1}$ directly to the next
phase and compares canonical bytes at the transition.  The hash is recomputed
for evidence but is not used as the equality premise.
\end{proof}

\section{Presently verified section re-entry}

A digest alone is not an anchor.  Re-entry at position $j$ requires a
\emph{VerifiedPrefixAnchor} containing the exact route, epoch, entrypoint,
index $j-1$, boundary type $X_j$, boundary value $x_j$, predecessor digest,
and a present prefix-verification receipt.  An independently supplied
certificate is admissible only after its exact route, epoch, boundary, and
predecessor relation are currently verified.  Orphan, stale, copied,
cross-route, or cross-epoch anchors reject.

\begin{proposition}[Section agreement]
Verification from a presently verified prefix anchor returns the same
terminal link digest as the corresponding whole-chain segment.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Both executions start with the same exact boundary value and predecessor, then
apply the same canonical link equation and fixed evaluator at every position.
Induction over the section length gives the same digest sequence.
\end{proof}

\section{Datatype and aperture boundary}

The patent fidelity ladder
\[
\mathsf{sint16}\to\mathsf{sint8}\to\mathsf{sint4}
\to\mathsf{sint2}\to\mathsf{sint1}
\]
is distinct from the raw observation-aperture ladder
\[
1,2,4,8,16\ \text{bytes}.
\]
No route phase has a universal signed-int16 premise.  Clipping, saturation,
wrapping, truncation, lossy quantization, collision acceptance, and silent
cross-type coercion are excluded from the exact boundary.

\section{Main theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Exact typed phase-chain closure]
Fix a valid epoch $A$, exact companion contracts, canonical operation tensor
$D_{\mathrm{op}}$, selector $s$, target $t$, fixed route, and well-typed
deterministic phase declarations.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item every admitted transition has one canonical link body and digest;
\item local verification accepts only its exact declared relation;
\item whole-chain verification accepts only the fixed ordered route with exact
canonical-byte continuity;
\item deletion, insertion, reordering, duplication, cross-route or cross-epoch
use, entrypoint or phase substitution, type or value mutation, operation
operand, selector or target mutation, operator or evaluator mutation, terminal
mutation, and trailing data reject;
\item a failed phase produces no admitted aggregate chain;
\item an accepted route returns exactly the result of Equations
(1)--(10) and \eqref{eq:manifest}, with receipt $B^*$, epoch, selector,
selected length, digest, and final bytes all equal to the dense Resolve result;
\item the independent window section returns exactly its declared window and
continuation-set pair and is never substituted for Resolve or manifestation;
\item evidence alone cannot restore any phase value;
\item a presently verified section agrees with whole-chain verification;
\item exact equality is independent of digest equality;
\item stored records, old receipts, caches, and prior success do not provide
present acceptance; and
\item no retained boundary vector or alternate mapback is admitted.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The table and phase equations prove exact composability.  Wire data identity
and Ionizer encode/decode identity preserve the data operand, operation
operand, and result tensor.  The two Wire route/digest states are separately
typed and linked; exact operation egress proves that the second canonical
operand completed its own unchanged crossing rather than bypassing transport.
Resolve and Manifest use the same $(D_{\mathrm{out}},s)$.  The manifestation
 receipt must bind $B^*$, epoch $A$, selector $s$, target $t$, exact selected length and
digest, and $\operatorname{ReadFinal}_N(t)=C_s$; exact final bytes are
compared directly.

Canonical link determinism follows from fixed declarations, canonical field
order, minimal scalar encodings, and deterministic digest input.  Streaming
verification establishes adjacency by byte equality and fresh evaluation.
Induction over route position proves ordered continuity; every listed mutation
changes a checked field, type, value, relation, position, predecessor, length,
or terminal comparison.  The verifier's two-value lifetime prevents a
boundary ledger.  Section agreement follows from the verified-prefix
proposition.  Codomain separation proves the independent Origamold clause.
\end{proof}

\section{Position relative to established constructions}

Hash chains and Merkle-style commitments bind ordered evidence
\cite{merkle1988}; session types describe communication structure and
compatibility \cite{honda2008}; verified codecs establish precise parser and
encoder relations \cite{delaware2019}; and state-machine replication studies
ordered transitions under different fault models \cite{lamport1982}.  The
present theorem is narrower: it fixes one heterogeneous tensor route, makes
every companion signature explicit, separates exact equality from digest
evidence, and excludes any retained inverse ledger.

\section{Scope}

The theorem establishes exact typed route closure and mutation-detecting
evidence for the declared epoch and route.  It does not establish objective
truth, permission, confidentiality, availability, calculation-engine safety,
network delivery, proof-system soundness beyond its premises, physical
erasure, or correctness outside the route.  Internal cross-repository
synchronization is not a premise of the mathematics.

\section{Conclusion}

An exact phase chain is a typed composition, not a label sequence.  The
construction fixes every boundary, carries both canonical calculation operands
through complete Wire routes, recomputes every relation, and uses hashes only
as evidence.
The main route and bounded tensor section now preserve the exact codomains of
Ionizer, Wire, Origamold, and Energizer without inventing a bridge or alternate
mapback.
